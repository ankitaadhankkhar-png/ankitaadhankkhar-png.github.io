About me

I wanted to design rooms. Now I think about how organisations design the way they use our data.

Law wasn't my first plan. As a teenager I wanted to be an interior designer, and law came along as the practical choice. It turned out to suit me far better than I expected. I started out drawn to family and criminal law, the most human side of legal practice.

Over time my attention moved to a different kind of design: how organisations collect, use and protect information about people. Privacy by design, it turns out, is still design.

That brought me to Europe. The GDPR and the AI Act were written here and have shaped laws far beyond the EU, and I admire their citizen-first approach. Today I study data law on the Erasmus Mundus programme, and I write about what new rules mean in practice.

Off the clock, I'm still an interior designer at heart: I notice layouts everywhere. I'm also learning German, one Artikel at a time.
